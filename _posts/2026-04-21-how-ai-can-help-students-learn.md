Since the introduction of AI and its rapid development, there has been a lot of controversy over the last couple of years about its uses and pitfalls. One crowd of people that could very well benefit from its use more than others is the current generation in college. There are two sides to AI: one that sees it as automatically cheating, and the other in which people use it for everything without a second thought. I think the truth is somewhere in the middle because AI can be very helpful to students, but only when it’s used responsibly, and not as a shortcut.

One of the biggest ways AI can help students is by making confusing concepts easier to understand. Everyone will eventually run into that one teacher who knows their subject but overcomplicates it, or the way something is worded in a textbook or article is confusing and hard to understand. AI can break it down for you into simpler language, making it easier to understand while also giving you examples or breaking it down step by step. I used it this way before when I was taking my beginning network class, and asked it to break down the seven layers of networking to make it easier to understand. Having the AI break each layer down in a simpler way allowed me to follow it more easily than looking at the textbook.
Another way AI can be useful is for brainstorming ideas. I have personally used it before to come up with game ideas and mechanic combinations, and that is a good example of using AI to help creativity instead of replacing, because to get something like that right, I was constantly going back and forth with the AI to get it just right and make sure it works within the game idea I was trying to build. The AI didn’t create the project or idea for me, but helped to expand on it and got me unstuck. This is where I think AI works the best, because it helps you get started and understand something while also organizing your thoughts rather than doing everything for you.

At the same time, I think students still need to be careful when using AI because it could also make learning and remembering materials worse if you become too dependent on it. I first noticed this firsthand when I was using AI to help me with my personal computer science projects. I started relying on it to do some basic coding and was forgetting the basic fundamentals and syntax, so I forced myself to stop using AI for the moment and essentially re-teach myself some basic syntax again. That’s when I realized that AI wasn’t helping but taking over my own learning and thinking. It replaces the part where you struggle and think, making you rely on the AI to remember for you.

<figure class="post-inline-graphic">
  <img src="/assets/images/posts/post-01/Infographic%2003.png" alt="Infographic with practical steps for students to use AI responsibly">

The advice I would give to other students, especially freshmen, is to at least try the work yourself, and then, if you get stuck, use AI to better understand the parts you’re stuck on, but never get the full answers. Another thing you could do is to rewrite things in your own words to make sure you understand the material yourself, rather than blindly following what the AI is doing. I do not believe students should be afraid of AI or immediately consider it as cheating, but I do think it needs to be used responsibly because if used the right way, it could support your learning, but if used the wrong way, it could replace it.
